# Silent Node: 3-Minute Demonstration Video Script
## Operational Walkthrough for Vector-Control Directors & Medical Officers of Health

**Runtime**: Exactly 3 Minutes (180 Seconds)  
**System Title**: Silent Node (Araksha v1.0.0)  
**Setting**: Screen capture of the web application running in browser alongside presenter voiceover  
**Important Corrections Enforced**:
- Header tab navigation is explicitly referenced as **"Docs & Evaluation"** (NOT "Docs & Verification").
- 2017 total notified cases in our surveillance panel are cited as exactly **174,687 cases** (NOT "over 186,000").
- Backtest metrics report verified values: Horizon $h=2$ Ensemble WIS **11.54** vs. Seasonal-Naive **15.76** (**26.8% reduction**; Fallback Gate: **PASS**).
- Interval dispersion limitation is stated openly: nominal 95% coverage is **77.9%** (< 80%), indicating under-dispersed intervals during explosive epidemic surges.

### [0:00 – 0:35] Scene 1: The Operational Challenge & Core Philosophy

**Visual**:  
Open on the Silent Node dashboard (`localhost:3000`). The camera zooms slightly into the top banner:  
`"Operational Planning Notice: Silent Node is a planning decision-support tool for district vector-control & Medical Officers of Health (MOH), NOT a clinical diagnostic tool."`  
The mouse cursor hovers over the system title "ARAKSHA v1.0.0 - 26 Reporting Units".

**Voiceover**:  
"Welcome to Silent Node, Sri Lanka's operational dengue risk forecasting system.

Every year, district vector-control teams and Medical Officers of Health face a critical operational window: chemical larviciding, premises inspections, and fogging teams require two to four weeks of logistical preparation to interrupt mosquito breeding cycles before hospital admissions peak.

Traditional surveillance looks in the rearview mirror. Furthermore, recent surveillance data is delayed—hospital notification logs take one to two weeks to consolidate. Silent Node solves this by enforcing a strict two-week reporting delay buffer: we never use data that wouldn't have been available at the forecast origin, eliminating optimistic leakage.

Across all 26 reporting units of Sri Lanka—including the independent Kalmunai health zone—Silent Node delivers actionable, four-week-ahead probabilistic risk forecasts."

### [0:35 – 1:15] Scene 2: Interactive Map, Horizon Slider & Historical Origin

**Visual**:  
Presenter moves the mouse to the **Scenario Switcher** in the subheader.  
Clicks the scenario selector and switches from `Latest Operational (2024-W20)` to `Historical Benchmark: May 2017 (Pre-Outbreak Peak)`.  
The choropleth map updates: the Western Province (Colombo, Gampaha, Kalutara) and Southern Province districts shift into amber and deep crimson tiers.  
Presenter clicks the **Horizon Toggle** from `1W Ahead` to `2W Ahead`, `3W Ahead`, and `4W Ahead`. The map shows escalating risk across the metro belt.

**Voiceover**:  
"Let’s see the system in action by stepping back to May 2017, eight weeks before Sri Lanka's largest dengue outbreak in history peaked in Week 29 at 10,590 cases in a single week—part of a nationwide panel total of 174,687 notified cases that year.

Notice how the map dynamically updates as we advance our planning horizon from one week to four weeks ahead. At the two-week horizon, Colombo and Gampaha are already flagged in 'Extreme' risk, with projected incidences exceeding 50 cases per 100,000.

Notice also the flame icon: Silent Node’s early anomaly detector flags impending outbreaks where projected caseloads exceed double the seasonal baseline while current clinical presentations are still quiet. This gives field officers a two-week head start before emergency rooms are overwhelmed."

### [1:15 – 1:55] Scene 3: Calibrated Fan Chart & Allocation Matrix CSV Export

**Visual**:  
Presenter clicks on the **Colombo** district card on the map.  
The right-hand panel displays the **Probabilistic Forecast Fan Chart**.  
The cursor traces the shaded prediction intervals:
- Center line: Median forecast ($q_{0.5}$)
- Inner band: 50% interquartile range ($q_{0.25}$ to $q_{0.75}$)
- Outer band: 95% interval ($q_{0.025}$ to $q_{0.975}$)  
Presenter points out that all quantiles are mathematically monotonic and non-crossing.  
Presenter scrolls down to the **Vector-Control Allocation Matrix** table. Points out the ranked Priority Rank, Incidence, Exceedance Probability, and Allocation Score.  
Clicks the blue **"Export Allocation CSV"** button; the browser downloads `silentnode_allocation_matrix_2017-05-01.csv`.

**Voiceover**:  
"Clicking Colombo opens our probabilistic forecast fan chart. These aren't hand-tuned guesses or arbitrary multipliers—they are real parametric quantiles generated by our negative-binomial generalized additive model and seasonal autoregressive ensemble, enforced through non-crossing isotonic projection.

Below the chart sits the Vector-Control Allocation Matrix. It ranks all 26 districts by a combined risk score incorporating projected incidence, probability of exceeding the historical 95th percentile, and local piped-water vulnerability from the CPH-2024 census.

With one click on 'Export Allocation CSV', vector-control coordinators receive a complete, deployment-ready spreadsheet to dispatch mobile fogging and Bti larvicide units during Monday morning logistics briefings."

### [1:55 – 2:35] Scene 4: The Fail-Safe & The "Docs & Evaluation" Tab

**Visual**:  
Presenter returns to the scenario bar and selects `Missing Recent Inputs (Fail-Safe Demo)`.  
The map immediately renders several districts in slate gray with the label `'Insufficient Data'`.  
Presenter points to the badge: "Never defaults to Low risk."  
Presenter navigates to the top-right header and clicks the tab button: **"Docs & Evaluation"**.  
The view switches to `DocumentationView`.  
Presenter shows the green banner: `Fallback Rule Gate: PASS`.  
The table highlights the backtest benchmark:
- Ensemble ($h=2$): WIS **11.54**, Median AE **18.76**, 50% Coverage **36.7%**, 95% Coverage **77.9%**, ROC-AUC **0.745**.
- Seasonal-Naive: WIS **15.76**, Median AE **25.57**.
- Metro breakdown: Colombo WIS **40.86** vs 60.26 (32.2% reduction); Gampaha WIS **29.70** vs 43.88 (32.3% reduction).  
Presenter hovers over the amber limitation callout at the bottom of the table.

**Voiceover**:  
"A core tenet of our design is absolute integrity. If a district's recent surveillance or weather feeds fail, Silent Node triggers an explicit 'Insufficient Data' fail-safe. It never defaults missing data to low risk.

Now let’s inspect the **Docs & Evaluation** tab. Here, every decision is fully transparent. In our rolling-origin backtest evaluated across the 2022 to 2024 epidemic seasons, our operational Ensemble achieved a Weighted Interval Score of 11.54 at the two-week horizon, beating the Seasonal-Naive baseline of 15.76 by 26.8%. In the Colombo metro hotspot, error was reduced by 32.2%.

We also report limitations honestly: our nominal 95% interval coverage reaches 77.9% at horizon two. Because exponential outbreak surges can outpace negative-binomial dispersion, our intervals are slightly under-dispersed—a limitation we state openly so officers treat the upper bound as a conservative baseline."
